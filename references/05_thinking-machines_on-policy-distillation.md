## Introduction

LLMs are capable of expert performance in focused domains, a result of several capabilities stacked together: perception of input, knowledge retrieval, plan selection, and reliable execution. This requires a stack of training approaches:

- **Pre-training** teaches general capacities such as language use, broad reasoning, and world knowledge.
- **Mid-training** imparts domain knowledge, such as code, medical databases, or internal company documents.
- **Post-training** elicits targeted behavior, such as instruction following, reasoning through math problems, or chat.

Smaller models with stronger training often outperform larger, generalist models in their trained domains of expertise.

Approaches to post-training a "student" model can be divided into two kinds:

- **On-policy training** samples rollouts from the student model itself, and assigns them some reward.
- **Off-policy training** relies on target outputs from some external source that the student learns to imitate.

We can use **distillation:** training the student to match the output distribution of a teacher model. The drawback of off-policy training is that the student learns in contexts frequented by the teachers, not ones the student itself will often find itself in. This can cause **compounding error**.

> If you're learning to play chess, on-policy RL is analogous to playing games with no coaching. Off-policy distillation is analogous to watching a grandmaster playing — you observe extremely strong chess moves, but they are played in board states that a novice player will rarely find themselves in.
>
> We want to combine the on-policy relevance of RL with the dense reward signal of distillation. For learning chess, this would be a teacher that grades each of _your own_ moves on a scale from "blunder" to "brilliant". For LLM post-training, it's on-policy distillation.

## On-policy distillation — best of both worlds

The core idea of on-policy distillation is to **sample trajectories from the _student_ model and use a high-performing teacher to grade _each token_ of each trajectory**.

| Method | Sampling | Reward signal |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Supervised finetuning | off-policy | dense |
| Reinforcement learning | on-policy | sparse |
| On-policy distillation | on-policy | dense |

Inspirations: [DAGGER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686), [process reward modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050). Extends prior work by [Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649), [Gu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543), and [the Qwen3 team](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388).

## Implementation

### Loss function: reverse KL

For simplicity, we choose the **per-token reverse KL** — the divergence between the student's ($\pi_\theta$) and teacher's ($\pi_\text{teacher}$) distribution for each token conditioned on the same prior trajectory:

$$\text{KL}\Bigl(\pi_\theta \,\big\|\, \pi_\text{teacher}\Bigr) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim {\pi_\theta}} \Bigl[ \log \pi_\theta(x_{t+1} \mid x_{1..t}) - \log \pi_\text{teacher}(x_{t+1} \mid x_{1..t}) \Bigr]$$

Useful properties of reverse KL:
- **Unhackable**: low KL always corresponds to high probability of desirable behavior.
- **Mode seeking**: learns one specific behavior (the teacher's) instead of spreading across suboptimal options.
- **Reduces exposure bias.**

Compute savings: doesn't require rollouts to finish, can use shorter / partial rollouts. Teacher only needs a single forward pass for log-probs while trajectories are generated by the cheaper student.

### Pseudocode

```python
# Initialize teacher client (main):
teacher_client = service_client.create_sampling_client(
    base_model=teacher_config.base_model,
    model_path=teacher_config.load_checkpoint_path,
)

# Sample trajectories (main):
trajectories = do_group_rollout(student_client, env_group_builder)
sampled_logprobs = trajectories.loss_fn_inputs["logprobs"]

# Compute reward (compute_teacher_reverse_kl):
teacher_logprobs = teacher_client.compute_logprobs(trajectories)
reverse_kl = sampled_logprobs - teacher_logprobs
trajectories["advantages"] = -reverse_kl

# Train with RL (train_step):
training_client.forward_backward(trajectories, loss_fn="importance_sampling")
```

It's literally a one-line change on top of RL with KL regularization — swap out the regularizer model.

## Distillation for reasoning

Used Qwen3-8B-Base as student, Qwen3-32B as teacher. Mid-train: SFT on OpenThoughts-3 (400K prompts from QwQ-32B) → AIME'24 = 60%.

| Method | AIME'24 | GPQA-Diamond | GPU Hours |
|--------|---------|--------------|-----------|
| Off-policy distillation (Qwen3 report) | 55.0% | 55.6% | — |
| + Reinforcement learning | 67.6% | 61.3% | 17,920 |
| + On-policy distillation | 74.4% | 63.3% | **1,800** |

Replicated: starting from 400K SFT checkpoint, on-policy distillation achieves **70% on AIME'24 in ~150 steps** (~77K prompts × 4 samples).

| Method | AIME'24 | Teacher FLOPs | Student FLOPs | Cost vs SFT-2M |
|--------|---------|--------------|--------------|---------------|
| Init: SFT-400K | 60% | 8.5×10²⁰ | 3.8×10²⁰ | – |
| SFT-2M (extrapolated) | ~70% | 3.4×10²¹ | 1.5×10²¹ | 1× |
| RL | 68% | – | – | ≈1× |
| **On-policy distillation** | **70%** | 8.4×10¹⁹ | 8.2×10¹⁹ | **9–30×** |

**9× cost reduction** if SFT data is given; **30×** if you have to sample fresh from the teacher.

## Distillation for personalization

Goal: build an internal company assistant with both knowledge of company docs and strong instruction-following.

Problem: Training on new docs with SFT degrades original post-training behaviors (IF-eval drops). Even mixing in 100% chat data still regresses. LoRA helps marginally but learns less.

Solution: After mid-training on docs, run **on-policy distillation with the earlier version of the model (Qwen3-8B) as teacher** on Tulu3 prompts. No relation to internal docs — purely recovers instruction following.

| Model | Internal QA (Knowledge) | IF-eval (Chat) |
|-------|------------------------|---------------|
| _Qwen3-8B_ | 18% | 85% |
| _+ midtrain (100%)_ | 43% | 45% |
| + midtrain (70%) | 36% | 79% |
| **+ midtrain (70%) + distill** | **41%** | **83%** |

The model itself is treated as a reward model — high-probability behaviors are rewarded. Has connections to inverse RL.

## Discussion

### Dense supervision greatly improves compute efficiency

> In [LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/), we presented the information-theoretic perspective that **reinforcement learning only teaches $O(1)$ bits per episode**. In contrast, **distillation teaches $O(N)$ bits per episode**, where $N$ is the number of tokens.

Experiment: start Qwen3-8B-Base, run RL on DeepMath (teacher), then distill back to base. Distillation reaches teacher performance in ~7-10× fewer gradient steps; total compute reduction **50–100×**.

### Distillation can effectively reuse training data

Trained on **a single prompt** for 20 steps × 256 rollouts (5,120 graded sequences) and nearly matched teacher AIME'24 performance.

> Prompt: "Evaluate the limit: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \sqrt{x} \left( \sqrt[3]{x+1} - \sqrt[3]{x-1} \right)$"

### RL searches in the space of semantic strategies

> RL doesn't spend a lot of compute on the gradient steps themselves. We should think of RL as spending most of its compute on _search_ — rolling out a policy and assigning credit — rather than on making updates. … Once a good strategy is found, distillation serves as a shortcut for learning it.

### On-policy learning as a tool for continual learning

Even SFT on a model's *own* samples (KL=0 against itself in expectation) degrades IF-eval, because each finite batch is slightly off-policy. On-policy distillation stays on-policy throughout because the teacher is fixed.

---

## Conclusion

> By leveraging on-policy sampling from the student with dense supervision from a teacher, the on-policy distillation recipe reaches frontier capabilities at a fraction of the cost of frontier high-compute RL runs.
